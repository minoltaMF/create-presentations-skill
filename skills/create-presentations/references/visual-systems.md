# Visual systems

Define a deck with a small Design DNA rather than a long list of decorative adjectives.

## Design DNA

Specify:

- aspect ratio and safe area;
- palette and contrast behavior;
- typography mood and fallback fonts;
- density and whitespace rhythm;
- image, illustration, diagram, and chart treatment;
- title and page-number behavior;
- recurring motif, grid, and alignment logic;
- composition freedom from restrained to cinematic.

## Style families

Use these as starting families, then adapt them to the topic and audience:

- editorial paper;
- cinematic visual narrative;
- clean professional;
- consulting/data story;
- scientific/technical;
- classroom/explainer;
- hand-drawn whiteboard;
- retro editorial;
- warm craft;
- bold poster;
- minimal monochrome;
- product launch;
- photographic documentary;
- brand-template faithful.

Do not present near-duplicate styles as separate choices. A useful option must differ in composition, typography, image treatment, and emotional tone.

## Page-role variety

Vary the silhouette while keeping the system coherent:

- minimal cover;
- thesis or statement;
- chapter transition;
- split comparison;
- sequence or process;
- evidence with source;
- data or chart;
- case or screenshot;
- quote or testimonial;
- visual full bleed;
- recap and action.

Avoid turning every page into a dashboard, card grid, or UI panel. Use icons only when they improve recognition; do not substitute ambiguous icons for essential labels.

## User references

When the user supplies a deck, image, PDF, website, mood board, logo, font, or brand guide:

1. inspect it before proposing a style;
2. identify repeatable tokens and page structures;
3. preserve licensed assets and brand constraints;
4. distinguish faithful following from loose inspiration;
5. do not claim pixel fidelity until representative pages are rendered and compared.
